Myth or fact?

Does fast charging ruin your battery?

Short answer: mostly myth. The real damage comes from something else. Swipe →

How a charge fills up

A battery takes charge fast when it's low, slowly when it's nearly full.

Fast chargers push most of their power into the empty half of the battery, then ease off.

Why it's mostly fine

Your phone, not the charger, decides how fast it charges.

A battery built for fast charging accepts it easily up to about half full. After that, the charge current is turned down.

Battery University: most chemistries accept a fast charge from empty to about 50% "with little losses."

The real enemy

It's not speed. It's temperature.

Too hot

Heat speeds up permanent capacity loss. Above 30°C (86°F) already counts as "elevated."

Too cold

Fast charging a cold battery can plate lithium metal on the anode.

Battery University: "Do not apply a fast charge when the battery is cold or hot."

Where wear does happen

The extra stress is in the last stretch to 100%.

The second half of a charge is where acceptance gets "labored," and pushing hard there causes the wear.

That's why fast chargers taper near full, and why stopping around 80% is gentler on any battery.

Do this

Fast charge the smart way

  • 1Use the charger your device supports. It controls the speed.
  • 2Charge off the pillow, out of the sun, case off if it gets hot.
  • 3Freezing cold device? Let it warm to room temperature first.
  • 4No rush? Slow charging overnight is gentler still.
The verdict

Mostly myth. Fast charging is fine. Heat and cold are what age it.
